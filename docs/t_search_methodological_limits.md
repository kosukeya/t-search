# t-search Methodological Limits

## Status

Finalization analysis of the methodological limits exposed by the merged Stage 1–16 research program.

Finalization baseline:

`main` merge commit `477a8e940bfcfaab377d618f7512027bacb5b5dd` after PR #17.

Companion finalization artifacts:

- `docs/t_search_closure_decision.md`;
- `docs/t_search_final_synthesis_protocol.md`;
- `results/t_search_final_claim_ledger.md`;
- `results/t_search_final_synthesis.md`.

This document introduces **no new scientific evidence**. It analyzes why the methods used by t-search are no longer sufficient, by themselves, to justify stronger claims about blockness or ontological becoming.

`methodological-limits analysis != new experiment`.

`project closure decision != physical theorem`.

`repository validation != new scientific evidence`.

---

# 1. Purpose

The main scientific value of t-search was not that it found one final mathematical signature of blockness or becoming. Its value was that it progressively exposed how easy it is to mistake a property of a representation, interface, gauge choice, clock choice, path description, basis, locality convention, or bounded search family for a property of reality itself.

The project therefore ends with a methodological question sharper than the one with which it began:

> **What additional principles would be required before a finite-model structural difference could legitimately be interpreted as a physical difference between blockness and ontological becoming?**

The merged Stage 1–16 record reveals five central limits:

1. **admissibility / equivalence-class dependence** — an invariant is meaningful only relative to a justified class of transformations;
2. **finite-model extrapolation** — finite graph and finite constraint precursors do not automatically inherit the physical meaning of continuum spacetime or general relativity;
3. **operational-to-ontological underdetermination** — operational equivalence or covariance does not uniquely determine ontology;
4. **missing bridge criterion for ontological becoming** — no tested structural signature was independently shown to be sufficient for becoming;
5. **search-family regress** — enlarging a bounded search repeatedly can improve coverage without converging to a physically privileged theorem.

These limits interact. None can safely be treated as merely an engineering inconvenience.

---

# 2. What the t-search method was good at

Before describing the limits, it is important to separate them from the strengths of the program.

The staged method was effective at **diagnostic separation**.

Across Stage 1–16 it repeatedly asked whether one apparent temporal feature survives when a competing explanatory resource is changed while other ingredients are held fixed as far as possible.

Examples include:

- incomplete local information versus global reconstructibility;
- hidden-selected versus no-selected-future semantics;
- order versus record asymmetry;
- stationary global constraint states versus relational change;
- one internal clock versus another;
- current record content versus directional record structure;
- continuation multiplicity versus modal semantics and weights;
- numerical reconstructibility versus typed operational identification;
- external parameterization versus internal clock perspective;
- gauge representatives versus physical orbit classes;
- raw path differences versus compensated quotient descent;
- noncommuting constraint presentations versus equivalent commuting bases;
- simple scalar trivialization versus richer triangular mixing;
- local/smeared presentations versus locality-preserving Abelianization;
- open-chain versus closed-cycle locality pressure.

This strategy was strong because it frequently turned an intuitive implication into an explicit counterexample or scope-limited result.

For example:

`order => record arrow`

was weakened by a no-record control;

`stationary global state => no change`

was weakened by relational clock dynamics;

`noncommuting presentation => fundamental non-Abelianity`

was weakened by equivalent commuting bases;

`local constraint structure => protected noncommutativity`

was weakened by the Stage 15 one-step L1 witness.

The method therefore succeeded as a **filter against premature ontological inference**.

Its limit appears when the project asks for something stronger than diagnostic separation: a representation-independent criterion with enough independently justified physical meaning to decide an ontological question.

# 3. Limit I — Admissibility and equivalence-class dependence

## 3.1 The general problem

Any claim that a feature is invariant requires a transformation class.

Formally, the phrase

`feature X is invariant`

is incomplete until one specifies

`invariant under transformation class A`.

The physical claim is stronger still. It requires some justification for why transformations in `A` count as redescriptions of the same physical situation rather than changes of physical situation.

Thus there are at least three logically distinct layers:

1. **mathematical transformation class** — what maps are formally allowed;
2. **equivalence convention** — which such maps are treated as descriptions of the same modeled content;
3. **physical admissibility principle** — why that equivalence convention should track sameness in the target physics.

The first can be defined inside a toy model. The second can be frozen as a protocol choice. The third normally requires an independent physical argument.

This distinction became increasingly important from Stage 13 onward.

## 3.2 Basis equivalence exposed the problem directly

Stage 13 constructed a noncommuting first-class presentation but also a tested equivalent commuting basis.

Stage 14 then made the pressure stronger: phase-space-dependent structure functions obstructed a declared diagonal scalar-rescaling class, yet a richer determinant-one triangular mixing produced a strongly commuting basis while preserving the tested quotient, Dirac pair, complete relational values, and inherited public operational content.

This sequence demonstrates why an obstruction relative to one transformation class cannot automatically be interpreted as physically fundamental.

A class may be too narrow.

Conversely, an extremely broad class can erase distinctions one might have had physical reasons to preserve.

So the methodological question becomes:

> **What makes a basis transformation admissible as a physical redescription?**

The finite algebra alone does not answer this.

## 3.3 Locality sharpens but does not solve admissibility

Stages 15–16 introduced explicit finite-graph locality conditions precisely to stop unrestricted/global basis transformations from settling the issue too cheaply.

That was a scientifically useful pressure test.

However, the declared locality classes remained model definitions.

In Stage 16, an L1-locality-preserving transformed generator had to satisfy locality of mixing, coefficient dependence, canonical support, invertibility, and inverse locality with respect to the four-cycle graph metric.

This is much stronger than merely requiring a sparse forward transformation.

Yet the project still did not establish:

`Stage 16 L1 locality = physically fundamental locality`.

The declared radius-1 graph neighborhood is not relativistic microcausality. A basis-locality rule is not automatically a causal principle. An inverse-locality requirement is a mathematically motivated equivalence condition, not an empirical law.

Therefore even a hypothetical full nonexistence theorem inside the Stage 16 L1 class would still require an additional argument before becoming a statement about physical locality.

`declared basis locality != uniquely privileged physical equivalence`.

## 3.4 The symmetry trap

A related danger appears when computational tractability motivates symmetry restrictions.

Stage 16D included a translation-covariant affine cyclic one-step L1 ansatz. The exact saturation/Groebner result genuinely excludes invertible strong solutions in that frozen ansatz.

But translation covariance of the **basis transformation** was an ansatz restriction. It was not established as a necessary physical property of every admissible local equivalence.

This illustrates a general methodological rule:

> A symmetry imposed to make a search finite or solvable must not be silently promoted into a physical necessity.

The same warning applies to polynomial degree bounds, finite composition depth, coefficient templates, chosen support radius, and other executable restrictions.

## 3.5 What would overcome this limit?

The admissibility problem would be materially improved by an independently motivated principle that fixes, or sharply constrains, the equivalence class.

Examples of the kind of development that would count include:

- a derivation of the allowed transformation class from a known physical symmetry principle;
- a locality notion inherited from a continuum theory with established causal meaning;
- a canonical geometric structure that determines admissible field/basis redefinitions;
- a theorem showing that all physically admissible maps reduce to a mathematically characterized class;
- an operational principle demonstrating that transformations outside the class change measurable content.

Merely choosing a larger or smaller class inside the finite carrier does not solve the problem.

# 4. Limit II — Finite-model extrapolation

## 4.1 Finite precursors are diagnostic, not automatically representative

The later t-search carriers were deliberately finite.

This made exact validation possible:

- finite physical-orbit quotients;
- exact path compensation;
- finite clock atlases;
- deterministic basis audits;
- exact symbolic bracket identities;
- complete enumeration inside declared search classes;
- exact Groebner/saturation certificates for restricted ansatz families.

These are real strengths.

But the same finiteness limits what can be inferred about physical spacetime.

A finite graph with a radius-1 locality notion does not automatically approximate relativistic locality merely because both use the word `local`.

A finite first-class constraint algebra does not become the hypersurface-deformation algebra merely because its brackets close.

A finite reparameterization atlas does not establish diffeomorphism invariance.

A multi-constraint path precursor does not establish refoliation invariance.

`finite graph locality != relativistic locality`.

`finite constraint precursor != general relativity`.

## 4.2 Structural analogy is not yet an embedding

Much of t-search intentionally used **precursor** language.

That language matters.

A precursor can isolate one structural ingredient of a more complicated theory without reproducing the full theory.

For example, a finite model may contain:

- multiple first-class directions;
- nontrivial structure functions;
- local/smeared generator labels;
- path compensation;
- quotient observables.

These ingredients can be useful for pressure-testing reasoning patterns associated with generally covariant systems.

But structural resemblance alone does not fix the physical interpretation of every mathematical feature.

To cross that boundary, one would need an embedding, limit, derivation, or other mathematically controlled relationship to the established target theory.

## 4.3 Topology in a finite carrier can be informative without being ontic

Stage 16F is an instructive example.

Opening the topology recovers local Abelianization witnesses:

- projected-open C3 recovers a one-step L1 witness;
- wrap-open C4 exhibits finite local depth 2;
- closed C4 has no witness in the declared Stage 16D local search.

This is a valid finite-family comparison.

It suggests that cycle closure is a useful discriminating variable **inside the tested construction**.

It does not establish:

`closed spatial topology => fundamental local obstruction`.

Several explanations remain live:

- a genuine cycle-related algebraic obstruction;
- the chosen locality rule interacting with the cycle;
- the finite carrier creating a special algebraic coincidence;
- the chosen search families missing a local witness;
- a broader invariant that has not yet been characterized.

Thus topology can be a meaningful model variable without yet being a fundamental ontological variable.

## 4.4 Scaling is not automatically extrapolation

One tempting response to finite-model uncertainty is simply to increase graph size.

That can be useful, but it does not by itself establish a continuum limit.

A sequence

`C4 -> C5 -> C6 -> ...`

may reveal a scaling pattern, yet the physical meaning of that pattern still depends on:

- what quantity is held fixed;
- how locality scales;
- how constraints scale;
- whether the family approximates a known continuum structure;
- whether the relevant equivalence class also has a controlled limit.

Larger finite models are therefore evidence about a larger finite family unless an explicit limiting argument is supplied.

`more sites != continuum spacetime by itself`.

# 5. Limit III — Operational equivalence does not determine ontology

## 5.1 Stage 2 exposed the problem early

Stage 2 constructed two formally distinct model families:

- an epistemic model with a globally selected complete history hidden from the local operational interface;
- an ontic-extension model with no selected complete future stored in the modeled state.

Under matched positive-support conditions, both could produce the same declared local operational outputs.

This gave the project an early and persistent warning:

`operational equality != ontological equivalence`.

But an equally important converse warning follows:

`operational indistinguishability != proof that ontology is forever unknowable`.

The correct conclusion is narrower: **the tested interface does not discriminate the two modeled semantics**.

## 5.2 Better covariance does not close the ontology gap

Later stages made the operational layer much richer.

The project tested:

- genuine clock changes;
- observable transport;
- record transport;
- continuation-specific states and weights;
- future-measurement effects and likelihoods;
- external reparameterization;
- gauge representative changes;
- quotient descent;
- multi-constraint path compensation;
- basis changes;
- typed O/P/R/V/Xi correspondence.

This increasingly strong covariance and descent structure did improve the reliability of statements such as:

`these descriptions ask the same declared operational question`

or

`these representatives descend to the same tested quotient payload`.

It did not supply a rule of the form:

`same operational content => same ontology`.

Nor did it supply the opposite rule:

`different internal formal semantics => physically different ontology`.

## 5.3 Future-measurement covariance is a particularly important example

Stage 10 established fully typed finite future-measurement covariance in its declared continuation-aware family.

That is stronger than merely matching a few probabilities.

It retained correspondence, normalization, continuation alignment, weighted predictions, and update structure across tested perspectives.

Still:

`future-measurement covariance != future actuality`.

The fact that one can consistently assign or transport probabilities concerning a future event does not answer whether the event is already actual, not yet actual, or represented by some ontology that does not fit either simple description.

A probabilistic structure can be shared across distinct interpretations.

## 5.4 Typed resources improve identification without becoming metaphysical primitives

Stages 10–16 increasingly retained typed resources `O/P/R/V/Xi` and provenance/correspondence data.

This prevented false equivalences caused by reusing the same numerical array while silently changing what it represented.

That was a methodological improvement.

But the necessity of a type label for correct model comparison does not imply that the type label names a fundamental constituent of reality.

`typed-resource necessity != metaphysical fundamentality`.

This distinction is general: semantic discipline in a formalism is not itself an ontology.

# 6. Limit IV — Missing bridge criterion for ontological becoming

## 6.1 The central missing implication

The project tested many structures that can look becoming-like:

- succession/order;
- local updates;
- Potentiality;
- record asymmetry;
- relational change;
- path ordering;
- raw path-word inequality;
- constraint noncommutativity;
- phase-space-dependent structure functions;
- failure of simple scalar trivialization;
- failure to find a local Abelianization witness;
- closed-cycle locality pressure.

But a structure can be mathematically dynamic, asymmetric, modal, noncommutative, or hard to trivialize without thereby satisfying a philosophically and physically justified criterion for **ontological becoming**.

The missing step is a bridge principle.

Schematic examples would be:

`structural property S => ontological becoming`

or perhaps

`S + independently justified physical assumptions A => ontological becoming`.

No such implication was established by Stage 1–16.

## 6.2 Relational change is not enough

The project repeatedly constructed nontrivial relational change alongside global, stationary, quotient, or Dirac-invariant descriptions.

Thus relational change is perfectly compatible, in the tested finite families, with structures often associated with block-like descriptions.

This does not refute becoming.

It shows only that:

`relational change != sufficient evidence for ontological becoming`.

## 6.3 Records are not enough

Record asymmetry gave a meaningful directional structure in Stage 3 and later constrained quantum variants.

But it was boundary-, ensemble-, and interface-sensitive, and could coexist with reversible microscopic dynamics.

No independent derivation connected it to:

- future non-actuality;
- objective passage;
- creation of facts;
- phenomenal passage;
- ontological becoming.

`record-defined direction != ontological future openness`.

## 6.4 Potentiality is not enough

The project explicitly represented no-selected-future semantics.

That was important because it made `ontic Potentiality` a formal model role rather than a verbal label.

Still, constructing a state description without a selected future does not establish that nature itself has no selected or already-actual future.

Likewise, a hidden-selected model is not evidence that nature has a fixed future.

The two are alternative model semantics until an independent discriminator is supplied.

## 6.5 Noncommutativity and path ordering are not enough

Stages 13–15 make this especially clear.

A noncommuting presented basis can have an equivalent commuting basis.

Raw path order differences can be compensated while quotient, relational, and typed content remains stable.

A scalar trivialization can fail while richer mixing succeeds.

Therefore the physically relevant question is not simply whether a presented algebra is noncommutative.

It is whether some noncommutative or path-dependent structure survives **all physically admissible redescriptions** and whether that surviving structure has an independently defended connection to becoming.

Neither half of that conjunction was established project-wide.

## 6.6 Local non-Abelianizability would still need interpretation

Suppose future work proved a genuine local non-Abelianizability theorem for a physically justified class.

That would be a substantial structural result.

Even then, t-search has not established:

`local non-Abelianizability => ontological becoming`.

One would still need to explain why inability to find a local commuting presentation corresponds to objective production, temporal passage, future non-actuality, or another defensible notion of becoming rather than to some different structural property.

This is why Stage 17, even if successful as a theorem-producing stage, would not by itself settle the original metaphysical question.

# 7. The symmetric limit — Missing bridge criterion for blockness

The project must apply the same standard to blockness.

Several structures can look block-like:

- a global mathematical description;
- stationary constrained states;
- exact reconstruction from local data;
- reversible clock transformations;
- perspective covariance;
- Dirac observables;
- physical quotient classes;
- complete relational observables;
- strongly commuting equivalent bases;
- global Abelianization.

But none by itself establishes:

- equal actuality of all events;
- eternalism;
- a metaphysically privileged global view;
- absence of objective becoming.

A global formal object is not automatically a physically instantiated God's-eye block.

Mathematical reconstructibility does not entail that reconstructed events are equally actual.

A commuting basis does not turn a dynamic ontology into a block ontology by definition.

`global Abelianization != proof of block ontology`.

`Dirac-invariant data + relational change != proof of eternalism`.

This symmetry is methodologically essential. Weakening naive arguments for becoming cannot be counted as positive evidence for blockness unless an independent bridge to block ontology is supplied.

# 8. Limit V — Search-family regress

## 8.1 The Stage 16 form of the problem

Stage 16D is the clearest place where computational completeness and physical completeness diverge.

The declared local search included:

- L0 candidates;
- 16 explicit one-step L1 elementary cyclic shears;
- all 69,904 elementary L1 compositions through depth 4;
- a frozen 12-parameter translation-covariant affine cyclic L1 ansatz.

No local strongly commuting witness was found in those declared families.

The affine class received an exact no-solution certificate for invertible strong solutions.

This is a legitimate bounded negative result.

But the conceptual L1 class was broader than those executable families.

Therefore the next move could always be:

- remove translation covariance;
- permit site-dependent coefficients;
- increase polynomial degree;
- allow more canonical-variable dependence;
- increase composition depth;
- enlarge the generator family;
- alter the normal form.

Each extension can produce a stronger bounded statement without necessarily approaching a physically complete one.

## 8.2 Why more computation is not automatically more discrimination

Suppose searches `A_1 subset A_2 subset A_3 ...` all return no witness.

This can increase confidence that simple witnesses are absent.

But for the ontological question, three additional conditions would still be required:

1. the union or limiting class must have a clear mathematical characterization;
2. that class must be physically justified as the relevant admissible equivalence class;
3. the resulting obstruction must have a justified bridge to the ontology under discussion.

Without these, computational coverage grows faster than ontological discrimination.

This is the key reason t-search freezes further bounded expansion as an end in itself.

## 8.3 Exact certificates are valuable but scope-sensitive

The project should not understate exact algebraic certificates.

The Stage 14 diagonal obstruction and Stage 16 affine cyclic no-solution result are stronger than random sampling failures.

They establish genuine class-level facts.

But exactness answers:

`is there a solution in this exactly specified class?`

It does not answer:

`is this class physically complete?`

These are different notions of completeness.

The distinction should be preserved explicitly:

- **solver completeness** — exact answer inside the encoded class;
- **ansatz completeness** — whether the encoded class exhausts a declared mathematical target class;
- **physical completeness** — whether that target class exhausts physically admissible redescriptions.

A proof at the first level cannot silently become a proof at the third.

## 8.4 Brute-force depth is especially prone to regress

Finite composition depth is useful for finding constructive witnesses and minimal exhibited complexity.

However, a failure through depth `d` does not generally provide a principled reason to stop at `d` rather than `d+1`.

If the underlying generated transformation family lacks a finite normal-form/completeness theorem, depth expansion naturally creates an open-ended sequence.

A future theoretically stronger program would therefore seek:

- a normal form;
- a finite generating invariant;
- a group/groupoid/semigroup characterization;
- a proof that depth can be bounded;
- or an obstruction invariant independent of explicit search depth.

That would change the epistemic type of the result from `search coverage` to `structural theorem`.

# 9. Three kinds of underdetermination exposed by t-search

The word `underdetermined` can hide different problems. The final project state benefits from separating at least three kinds.

## 9.1 Interface underdetermination

Different internal models can produce the same declared public/operational output.

Example pattern:

`different internal semantics -> same O(G)`.

This was explicit in Stage 2 and persisted conceptually through later typed covariance work.

The remedy is a richer or different discriminator, if one exists.

## 9.2 Representation underdetermination

Different clocks, gauges, paths, or bases can represent the same tested quotient/operational content.

Example pattern:

`different presentation -> same licensed quotient/public payload`.

The remedy is a justified invariant/equivalence structure.

## 9.3 Model-class underdetermination

A finite negative result can be explained either by a real obstruction or by incompleteness of the explored ansatz/search family.

Stage 16 is the primary case.

The remedy is a completeness theorem, constructive counterexample, or independently justified restriction.

## 9.4 Ontological underdetermination

Even after operational and structural relations are fixed, multiple metaphysical interpretations can remain compatible because no bridge principle selects among them.

This is the final blockness/becoming boundary.

The remedy, if possible, must come from a new physically and philosophically defended criterion, not merely more internal bookkeeping.

These kinds of underdetermination should not be conflated.

Solving one does not automatically solve the others.

# 10. Why Stage 16 is a meaningful endpoint rather than an arbitrary stopping point

Stage 16 was not chosen in advance as the final stage.

Historically, Stage 16G selected Stage 17 as the next evidence-ranked gate.

That remains part of the project record.

The closure decision came afterward, once the epistemic form of the remaining problem was examined.

The reason Stage 16 is a sensible endpoint is not that its residual anomaly is uniquely profound. It is that the residual makes the upstream methodological limits unusually visible.

The project now knows that:

- a global strongly commuting basis exists;
- declared local search families find no local witness;
- one restricted affine family has an exact no-solution certificate;
- opening the topology restores local witnesses in controls;
- the physical quotient, Dirac pair, complete relational observables, and typed public content remain well behaved.

The unresolved issue is therefore not merely `does the code work?` or `is the presented algebra noncommutative?`.

It is:

> **which locality-preserving equivalences should count as physically admissible, and what ontological significance would their obstruction have?**

Those questions cannot be answered by another unprincipled increase in search coverage.

That makes Stage 16 a natural methodological endpoint for the current program.

# 11. What project closure does not mean

The closure decision must not be read as any of the following.

## 11.1 It does not mean the Stage 16 residual is uninteresting

The closed/open topology comparison may still motivate future mathematical work.

It is retained as a bounded structural anomaly.

## 11.2 It does not mean no local witness exists

The project did not search the complete conceptual L1 class.

`no L1 witness in frozen search != no L1 Abelianization exists`.

## 11.3 It does not mean a local obstruction theorem cannot exist

A future theorem may characterize a broader or physically justified class.

The project simply does not currently possess such a theorem.

## 11.4 It does not mean becoming is false

Failure to establish a sufficient becoming criterion is not a refutation of becoming.

`not_established != false`.

## 11.5 It does not mean blockness is true

The success of global reconstructions, quotient descriptions, covariance, and Abelianization does not establish eternalism.

## 11.6 It does not mean Stage 17 was scientifically refuted

Stage 17 was historically selected and then not pursued after methodological review.

`Stage 17 not pursued != Stage 17 refuted`.

## 11.7 It does not mean the entire project was inconclusive in every respect

Many finite/model-bounded structural claims are established.

The underdetermination applies to the **central ontological discrimination**, not to every intermediate result.

# 12. What would count as a qualitatively new discriminator

The project may be reopened, but reopening should change the epistemic structure of the problem rather than merely expand the old search.

The closure decision recognizes several kinds of development that would qualify.

## 12.1 Physically justified admissibility principle

A new principle that identifies which transformations count as physical redescriptions would directly address the largest ambiguity in the basis/locality thread.

It should be justified independently of whether it produces the desired obstruction.

A rule chosen because it preserves a preferred conclusion would not solve the problem.

## 12.2 Full-class theorem or invariant

A theorem applying to an explicitly characterized complete mathematical class would escape the repeated `no witness up to bound B` pattern.

Examples of suitable theorem-types could include:

- a constructive Abelianization theorem;
- a no-go theorem;
- a holonomy/cocycle/cohomological obstruction;
- a normal-form classification;
- a graph-topological invariant tied to the transformation class.

The particular theorem need not favor either ontology.

## 12.3 Constructive witness that resolves the current residual

A principled local strongly commuting witness on the Stage 16 closed carrier would show that the current residual was a search-class artifact.

That would be scientifically useful even though it would not prove blockness.

## 12.4 Independently defended ontology bridge

A future theory might supply a defensible reason that some validated invariant bears on objective passage, fact creation, future non-actuality, equal actuality, or another precise ontology.

Such a bridge must be argued independently of the t-search toy models themselves.

Otherwise the project would risk defining becoming as whichever property happened not to trivialize.

## 12.5 Continuum or established-physics embedding

A controlled relationship to a theory with established physical interpretation could upgrade the relevance of finite precursor results.

This could involve:

- a continuum limit;
- a discretization theorem;
- an embedding into a known constrained field theory;
- a relation to relativistic causal locality;
- a well-motivated gravitational constraint construction.

The key is not simply greater realism. It is a mathematically controlled transfer of interpretation.

# 13. Design lessons for any successor research program

The t-search closure suggests several methodological rules for future work on blockness/becoming.

## 13.1 Define the ontology discriminator before optimizing the search

A future program should state what observable, invariant, or theorem would genuinely favor one ontology and why.

Without this, technical sophistication can outrun interpretive relevance.

## 13.2 Separate four questions explicitly

For any candidate structure `S`, ask:

1. **Existence:** does `S` occur in the model?
2. **Representation stability:** does `S` survive declared equivalent redescriptions?
3. **Physical relevance:** is that equivalence class physically justified?
4. **Ontological bridge:** why would the surviving `S` imply or support blockness/becoming?

Most apparent shortcuts collapse two or more of these questions.

## 13.3 Prefer theorem-producing steps over indefinitely growing enumerations

Enumeration is useful for discovery and falsification of simple conjectures.

Once repeated search expansion becomes the dominant activity, the next step should usually seek a structural characterization rather than a larger bound.

## 13.4 Use controls that can reverse the desired result

Stages 1–16 were most informative when controls were capable of invalidating the favored interpretation.

Future work should preserve outcome-neutral design.

For example, both of the following should count as informative:

- finding a local commuting witness;
- proving a no-go theorem in a justified class.

## 13.5 Keep operational and metaphysical vocabularies typed separately

Terms such as `Potentiality`, `Actuality`, `record`, `future`, `locality`, and `invariance` can carry metaphysical connotations.

A successor project should continue distinguishing:

- formal data type;
- operational role;
- mathematical semantics;
- metaphysical interpretation.

This is one of the strongest methodological gains of t-search.

---

# 14. Final methodological assessment

The present research program reaches a clear limit of **discriminating method**, not a demonstrated limit of mathematics or physics.

The merged Stage 1–16 work establishes many nontrivial finite-model facts and several useful bounded counterexamples to naive implications.

It also exposes a recurring hierarchy:

`raw feature`

`-> representation test`

`-> quotient / covariance / equivalence test`

`-> locality / admissibility test`

`-> physical interpretation`

`-> ontological bridge`.

The project became progressively stronger at the middle layers.

It did not acquire independent principles strong enough for the final two layers.

That is why the correct endpoint is not:

`blockness established`

or

`becoming established`.

It is:

> **the current t-search method can rigorously separate many representational and operational effects, but cannot by itself determine the physically privileged equivalence class or supply the bridge from surviving finite-model structure to blockness or ontological becoming.**

The decision to stop further bounded search therefore follows from the type of missing information, not from lack of computational capacity alone.

A larger search could answer a larger search question. It would not automatically answer the ontological question.

- `no L1 witness in frozen search != no L1 Abelianization exists`;
- `cycle opening changes graph topology != proof that topology is ontic`;
- `failure to Abelianize != ontological becoming`;
- `global Abelianization != proof of block ontology`;
- `finite constraint precursor != general relativity`;
- `Stage 17 not pursued != Stage 17 refuted`;
- `more depth/degree/candidates alone != reopening condition`;
- `repository validation != new scientific evidence`.

## Compact methodological boundary

> **t-search can test whether candidate temporal structures survive explicitly declared reconstructions, clocks, gauges, paths, bases, locality rules, and bounded transformation families. It cannot, without additional independent principles, determine which full transformation class is physically privileged or infer blockness/ontological becoming merely from survival or failure inside a finite precursor. That gap — rather than an unresolved computational search alone — is the principal methodological limit at closure.**
