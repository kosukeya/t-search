## Frozen normalization boundary

Stage 9C uses normalized reduced-state Euclidean Born probabilities. Stage 9D clock maps are generally non-Euclidean-unitary and preserve an induced physical metric.

Stage 10 therefore freezes a **decision problem**, not a positive answer.

Candidate representations include:

- chart-local POVMs with declared local conditional normalization;
- metric-aware effect forms satisfying `sum_o F_o=G` and evaluated by `z^dagger F_o z / z^dagger G z`.

The metric-aware dual transport law

`F_Y=S^{-dagger}F_XS^{-1}`

is a candidate to test, not a theorem imported into Stage 10.

The retained representation must reproduce the exact Stage 9C reference probabilities, use one declared normalization convention consistently, and expose wrong-normalization controls.

## Frozen strong covariance criterion

For every canonical continuation h, every genuine corresponding clock pair, and every typed outcome o, Stage 10 asks whether:

`p^h_source(o)=p^h_target(chi_outcome(o))`

within tolerance after validating event/class/outcome/basis/normalization typing.

Success requires more than probability equality:

- valid effect representation;
- valid normalization;
- positivity/completeness;
- direct/composed route consistency;
- explicit outcome identity;
- valid event/class correspondence;
- per-continuation equality before weighting.

`weighted equality != per-continuation measurement covariance`.

`effect covariance without outcome typing != full measurement covariance`.

## Frozen modal/weight layer

Only after per-continuation likelihood covariance is tested may continuation weights and Stage 9C modal models be restored.

The protocol requires explicit tests for:

- matched epistemic/ontic public predictions across perspectives;
- hidden h* swap remaining outside the public schema;
- weight mismatch remaining operationally visible across corresponding charts;
- perspective-consistent evidence conditioning/posteriors;
- selector-free ontic-extension update.

No result in this layer may be read as evidence for ontological becoming or ontic openness by itself.

## Frozen negative controls

The protocol requires at least:

1. bare-effect reuse;
2. wrong-continuation map;
3. swapped continuation classes;
4. swapped/misdeclared outcome correspondence;
5. e1/e2 anchor-target confusion;
6. wrong/missing event correspondence;
7. wrong normalization/metric;
8. weight/class misalignment;
9. removed outcome typing;
10. mixed normalization conventions.

Accidental equality on one special state does not count as a passed control.
